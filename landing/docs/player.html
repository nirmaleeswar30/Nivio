The player

Nivio plays everything with mpv, a fast player that handles nearly any format with hardware decoding. This page covers how to control it on each device and what every setting in the player does.

Servers and sources

When you press play, Nivio asks its sources for a stream and starts the first one that works. If a source fails or is too slow, Nivio moves on to the next by itself, trying direct streams before embedded web players.

To choose a server yourself, tap the circular arrows at the top right of the player. Each server shows as a card. Pick one and playback continues from where you were.

Touch gestures

Double-tap left or rightSkip back or forward by your seek step (10 seconds by default). Keep tapping to skip further.

Swipe up or down on the rightVolume. Past 100% it boosts the sound up to 200%.
PinchZoom the video to fill the screen, or back to fit
Long pressLock the screen. Long press again to unlock.

The lock blocks every touch except the long press that unlocks it, so a pocket or a toddler can't pause or skip. Tapping while locked reminds you how to unlock. Change the seek step in Settings → Playback → Seek Duration.

Episodes, intros and auto-play

  • Previous and next episode buttons sit beside play/pause for shows and anime.
  • Skip intro and outro buttons appear when Nivio knows where they are. Turn on Auto Skip Intro & Outro in Settings → Playback to skip them without asking.
  • Auto Play Next Episode starts the next one when the current episode ends. For anime, Auto Skip Filler passes over filler episodes.
  • Seek previews: when a server provides thumbnails, dragging the seek bar shows a preview of that moment.
  • Nivio remembers where you stopped and resumes there next time.

Player settings

Tap the gear in the player to open its settings. Changes apply straight away, and most are remembered for next time.

Decoder modes

ModeHow it worksWhen to use it
AutoThe best hardware decoder available, and the CPU if none worksAlmost always
HW+Direct hardware decodingFastest, easiest on the battery
HWHardware decoding with copy-backThe video glitches or shows artifacts on HW+
SWDecoding on the CPUThe video is black, green or corrupted on the hardware modes

The Decoder tile shows the decoder actually in use. Some formats, such as 10-bit H.264 anime releases, have no hardware support and always use the CPU.

Ambient mode

Android phones

Ambient mode fills the black bars around the video with a soft glow taken from the picture, a bit like a TV with backlighting. It works with the Best Fit display mode. Tap the Ambient tile to adjust it:

  • Style: Glow + edge fade (the default) softly fades the video's edges into the glow. Glow only keeps sharp video edges.
  • Blur strength: a slider from 0 to 10. At 0 the glow keeps some shapes from the picture. At 10, the default, it's the softest and shows just the colours.

Ambient mode is off while a video plays in true HDR or Dolby Vision, because those use a separate video surface it can't draw around.

Picture-in-Picture and background

Android

With PiP on Lost Visibility turned on, leaving the app shrinks the video into a floating window. Pause on Lost Visibility pauses instead. Both are in Settings → Playback. The screen stays awake while a video is playing.

Pressing back in the player can also keep the video going in a mini player while you browse.
